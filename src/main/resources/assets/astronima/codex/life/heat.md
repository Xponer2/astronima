There is no air outside a sealed room and nothing to conduct into out here — a hull can only
throw heat away as light. That single fact is the whole reason a habitat equilibrates cold
rather than freezing solid, and it is why the shape of a base means something twice over.

## Radiating against the sky

Loss goes as the **fourth power** of the hull's own temperature, against a sky sitting at
2.7 K — colder than anything you will ever stand next to. A warm hull sheds heat fast; a cold
one barely at all. What a face can actually **see** is most of the answer: a wall looking at
open sky radiates freely, and a wall pressed against rock a metre away exchanges with the rock
and nets almost nothing.

> **Burying a habitat keeps it warm. Exposing it makes it cold.** That is the mechanic, not a
> decoration on top of one.

## Burying changes the loss, it does not end it

Buried rock sits at roughly −60 °C — cold, but nothing like the 2.7 K sky. Push a wall into
rock and the loss stops being a fourth-power term against near-absolute-zero and becomes a
linear one against a merely cold neighbour. That is a far smaller number, which is why burying
works — and it is not zero, which is why insulation is not optional.

## Insulated plate

A bare machined hull plate lets roughly twenty times as much through as one built with
insulated plate. Area-weighted, because the two kinds of wall are heat paths in **parallel**:
insulating half a shell halves its conducted loss and no more. Doing half the job gets you
about half the benefit, exactly — no shortcut for the other half.

## What puts heat back

Two real sources, both already burning fuel you have already spent:

- **Crew metabolism.** A resting person puts out about a filament lamp's worth of heat; working
  harder puts out three or four times that — the same exertion the breathing model already
  charges you oxygen for. One body, one number, spent two ways.
- **A worked machine.** Crank a crusher or a forge and everything that goes into breaking rock
  or bending metal comes back out as heat, same as the person turning the handle.

Neither is a heater you place — they are what is already happening in a room you are working
in, now counted.

## Reading the room

The gas analyzer does not just say a temperature; a room holding steady at 260 K and one
falling through it need different answers. It names whichever lever has the most left in it —
bury an exposed shell before insulating a buried one, because open sky dwarfs everything else —
rather than leaving you to guess which of the three actually helps.

## What is not sealed

A vented cavity has no shell to hold a balance in: it just drifts toward the rock's own
temperature over about a minute, the same as any other pocket of gas open to an asteroid that
has no weather.
