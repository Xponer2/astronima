A sealed room does not fail by running out of oxygen. It fails by filling with carbon dioxide,
and it does that **first**.

## The scrubber

A lithium hydroxide cartridge holds 40 moles of carbon dioxide and swaps at 5% left. It absorbs
in proportion to what is already in the air, which has a consequence worth planning around: it
works hardest exactly when the room is worst, so a cartridge that lasted a week alone will not
last with company.

> Carbon dioxide is what gets you. The oxygen outlasts it.

## The candle

Sodium chlorate gives up its oxygen when heated — from 523 K, finished by 773 K. It is a real
piece of emergency equipment, and it has a real failure mode: past **873 K** the chloride starts
coming off as **chlorine**, which is a lung poison, in the room you were making breathable.

The window between "not hot enough" and "poisoning the room" is the whole skill of running one.

## Reading it

The **gas analyzer** {item:astronima:gas_analyzer} is what turns all of this from guessing into
reading. Partial pressures, not just total pressure — see [[start/breathing]] for why that
distinction is the one that kills people.

A generator indoors is a scrubber load, not just a power source: see [[power/sources]].
