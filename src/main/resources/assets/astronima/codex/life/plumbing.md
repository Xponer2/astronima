Gas moves down a pressure difference and stops when both ends match. That single sentence
explains most of what surprises people about this system.

## The fourth power

Flow through a pipe goes as the **fourth power of the bore**. Two thin pipes carry an eighth of
one thick one, and a valve at half open passes a **sixteenth** of the flow, not half — nearly all
of a valve's useful range is in the last part of its travel.

> The tightest valve on a run sets the whole run's flow. One half-shut valve anywhere is the
> whole run half-shut.

## The pieces

- A **port** opens a run into a room. Its dark bore must face the air you want to reach; the other
  five sides are plain plate. **A run needs two openings** — one opening connects nothing to
  nothing.
- A **pipe** only ever equalises. It cannot fill a tank above room pressure.
- A **pump** moves gas from its big face to its small face. The mouth is big because suction must
  be wider than discharge or the impeller starves. It is what gets a tank above room pressure.
- A **tank** holds far more than a room of the same size and survives the habitat
  depressurising, which is what makes it a buffer rather than storage. Rated to 3000 kPa; a pump
  stalls at the rating, so filling normally can never burst one.
- A **purge valve** dumps to vacuum. The loss is the point.

## When nothing happens

A bore facing rock reaches no air. A run with a single port has nowhere to move gas to. A pump
reporting *two outlets* has more than one room or tank on one face, and a pump has one of each.
A pump reporting *stalled* has filled the tank, which is success.
