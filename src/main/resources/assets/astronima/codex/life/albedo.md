Everything on [[life/heat]] is about a room losing heat it never wanted to lose. This is the
other direction: a room gaining heat it never asked for, from the one thing outside a habitat
that puts real watts in for free — the sun.

## A wall does not just lose heat. It can gain it too

Bare, machined hull plate is a fair absorber of sunlight — real oxidized aluminum-like plate
takes in roughly half of what lands on it. Point an exposed wall at the sun for a while and that
heat has nowhere else to go but into the room behind it, on top of whatever machines are already
doing. A surface base with a lot of bare, exposed wall really does cook at noon, for the same
reason a car left in the sun does.

## Why the paint is white

Real spacecraft thermal-control paint is white for a specific reason: {item:astronima:titania},
the same pigment ground for it here, is a poor absorber of visible sunlight — a painted surface
takes in roughly a fifth of what a bare one does. Less sunlight in means less heat the room has
to shed later, whatever else is going on inside it.

Both real properties of a wall at once, and independent of each other: a plate can be insulated,
painted, both, or neither. Insulation slows what leaks through a wall that touches rock; paint
decides how much sunlight an exposed wall takes in to begin with. Painting an insulated plate
does not replace the jacket, and insulating a painted one does not undo the pigment.

## What it will not do

Paint changes nothing about a buried wall — [[life/heat]]'s own figures for burying and
insulating a room are untouched, because a wall with rock behind it never sees the sun at all.
And it changes nothing at night: no sunlight is falling on anything, painted or not, so a room
built entirely of this holds exactly as it would without it until the sun is back up. It is a
real, useful answer to one real problem — a hot wall in daylight — not a second insulation.
