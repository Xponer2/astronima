A claim or a plate is proof; a ritual is a bet. Build a figure over a site whose richness you do
not fully know, and the field itself tells you whether you guessed right — no scripted outcome, no
dice, just the real draw the site can or cannot sustain.

## The vocabulary

Four parts, and only four: a **focus** (the altar itself, at the centre), **arms** (2–4 straight
lines of solid blocks radiating out from it, horizontal, up to six blocks long), **anchors** (a
real `asterium_grains` block, compressed to `asterium_block`, terminating every arm), and a
**boundary** (a closed ring of solid blocks one block past the longest arm). The smallest real
figure that can complete — two arms, three blocks each — looks like this from above, focus at the
centre, an anchor at the tip of each arm, any solid block everywhere else on the arms and the ring:

## Activating it

Right-click the altar once the figure is complete. The anchors are spent immediately, whether the
ritual that follows ever completes or not — the real cost already happened in gathering the
grains, not in the attempt itself. A complete figure starts a real, live draw on the field beneath
and around it; an incomplete one (a missing arm, a missing anchor, an open boundary) refuses to
start at all, and nothing is consumed.

## Watching it run

A running ritual draws on the real field at each anchor's own position for a real minute, exactly
the way a collector sweep does — check it with the field meter mid-run and it reads the same
depletion the ritual itself is causing, not a separate number invented on the side. If the site can
sustain the draw for the full duration, it completes and precipitates a real batch of
`asterium_grains` — more than patient sweeping alone could gather in the same minute. If the site
cannot sustain it, the ritual stalls: the field goes dead under a structure still trying to pull
from it, visible and honest, never a silent no-op.
