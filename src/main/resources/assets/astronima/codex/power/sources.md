Three ways to make electricity here, and none of them is strictly better than the others. They
differ in what they consume and what they do to the room you are standing in.

## The array — free, and not enough

One square metre in full sun makes **37 W**. A worked machine wants **250 W**. That is seven
panels per machine, and none of them work at night.

> Sunlight at 2.7 AU is a seventh of Earth's. That is the distance, not a penalty — and it is
> why every real outer-system probe carries an RTG instead.

Roof one over and it makes nothing at all. It needs open sky, and so does the retort.

## The generator — power, and the air it costs you

Burns methane at **25%** thermal efficiency. The catch is not the fuel: it is that every mole of
fuel burns two of oxygen and makes one of carbon dioxide, **in the room you are standing in**.

> It stalls on air before it stalls on fuel. A generator in a sealed room suffocates itself, and
> then you.

Run it outside and wire the power in, or feed the exhaust to a scrubber. See [[life/air]].

## The fuel cell — twice as far, and it gives water back

**60%** efficient, because there is no heat engine in the middle and therefore no Carnot limit to
pay. Every mole of hydrogen wants half a mole of oxygen and gives back a mole of water — on a
closed base that water is not a by-product, it is the point.

The catch is upstream: it needs pure hydrogen, and methane is something you can dig up.

## Storing it

A **power cell** banks joules for the dark half of the day. Cells are what make an array useful
at all: without one, energy that has nowhere to go is not made.

Whatever makes the power, getting it anywhere is [[electrical/sizing]] — and a badly sized run
does not drain your batteries faster, it just gets nothing done.
