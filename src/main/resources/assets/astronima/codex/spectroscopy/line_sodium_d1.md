589.6 nm — the longer-wavelength half of sodium's famous doublet, the same pair that gives old
low-pressure streetlights their distinctive orange-yellow glow. Sodium contamination is a real,
classic plague in laboratory spectroscopy — a trace of ordinary salt shows up unmistakably.

## Where it shows up here

Both Andromeda's bulge and M32 carry it, part of the same old-star absorption forest their shared
Fe I line sits inside. See [[spectroscopy/line_sodium_d2]] for its own inseparable twin: the two sit
only 0.6 nm apart, close enough that this instrument's own reading precision cannot always tell them
apart — an honest limit, not a hidden one. A real spectroscope resolves the two; this one behaves
closer to a much smaller instrument.
