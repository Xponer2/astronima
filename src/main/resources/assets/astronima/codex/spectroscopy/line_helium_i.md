587.6 nm — and a line with a genuinely strange history: helium was found here, in the Sun's own
spectrum, in 1868 — a full twenty-seven years before anyone found it on Earth. An element named
after the Greek word for the Sun itself, because for a while the Sun was the only place it was known
to exist.

## Where it shows up here

The Sun carries it, and so do the Orion Nebula and the Helix Nebula — both lit, one by a young hot
star and one by an exposed white dwarf, hot enough to strip electrons off helium as well as
hydrogen. The Pleiades carries it too, standing in for the hot B-type stars embedded in that
reflection nebula's own dust.
