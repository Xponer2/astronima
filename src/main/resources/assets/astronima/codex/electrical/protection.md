An overloaded run has no cheap failure of its own. Without protection the thing that gives is
the **conductor** — slowly, in whichever place its cooling was worst, which may be behind a
wall you built afterwards.

## Sized by the wire, not by itself

Both the fuse {item:astronima:power_fuse} and the breaker {item:astronima:power_breaker} give
up at **four fifths of what the run can carry**. That is the continuous-load rule real wiring
uses, and it means the same component trips at 11 A on standard iron and at 2.3 A on signal
wire.

> There is no such thing as the wrong-sized fuse here, because a fuse *is* a deliberately weak
> length of the same conductor. What there is, is the wrong-sized wire — see
> [[electrical/sizing]].

## Which one to use

- A **fuse** is spent and replaced. Cheap, four to a craft, and you can bury it in a wall.
- A **breaker** is thrown back on. It costs more, never needs replacing, and it doubles as the
  switch you throw to work on a live circuit.

**Put a breaker where you will be standing when something goes wrong.** A breaker behind a
bulkhead is a breaker you cannot throw, and that is the one placement decision this component
exists to make you take.

You cannot reset a breaker into a live fault. Throw it back on with the third machine still
running and it trips again inside a tick. Fix the circuit, then the lever holds.
