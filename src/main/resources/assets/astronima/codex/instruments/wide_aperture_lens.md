A telescope's base lens is not weak because it is cheaply made. It is weak because it is *small*,
and a small lens has a real, physical ceiling on what it can ever show you — no amount of skill at
aiming it changes that ceiling.

## Aperture is light, not magnification

What actually limits a telescope is how much of a faint object's own light it can gather in the
time you hold on target. A wider lens catches more of that light, the same way a wider bucket
catches more rain — it does not make the object bigger, it makes the object *visible at all*.
This is why a real backyard telescope shows Orion's Nebula easily and Andromeda as barely a smudge,
and why a cheap department-store scope shows you neither: aperture, not zoom, is the real
difference between them.

## What it actually opens

Every object in this catalogue has a real required aperture — [[sky/object_pleiades]],
[[sky/object_andromeda_galaxy]] and [[sky/object_m32]] all need more light than the base lens can
gather. The reticle still finds their direction with a base lens; it simply cannot complete a
capture there, and says so rather than failing silently. This lens is what closes that gap.
