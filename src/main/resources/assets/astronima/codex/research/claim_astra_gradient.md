Every reading anyone has ever taken with a field meter agrees on one thing: the number is never the
same twice, and it is never the same everywhere. Before this claim, that could still be an
instrument fault, or noise, or nothing at all. Two real readings, taken far enough apart, settle it.

## What this claim actually asks

A single measurement proves nothing about a gradient — it is one point, and one point has no slope.
Take a reading close to the core and another out at ordinary working depth or past it, and the same
pattern shows up everywhere on the body: astra is not a flat ambient hum, it thickens toward the
centre and thins toward the rim, the same radial shape the ore bands themselves already follow.

The second half of the claim is what a collector already demonstrates in the hand: draw on a rich
site twice in a row and the second reading comes back lower. The field is not signal, freely
available and undiminished by use — it is a real, finite resource, and it only recovers slowly, by
diffusion from richer ground nearby.

## The stages

A reading only counts once its two ends are both on record — take one near the core and one further
out before the first stage will move. The second stage asks you to own both real instruments this
tier has built so far, the meter that reads the field and the collector that draws on it, so you can
find the gradient again anywhere else on the body without guessing. The third spends two grains of
worked asterium to file the claim for good — the same way a spectral plate is spent elsewhere in
this book.
