Every real assay of a rock's metal content used to mean the same thing: crush it, heat it, and see
what burns off. Spectroscopy offers something a chemist a century ago would have called impossible —
naming an element from a distance, without ever touching the sample at all.

## What this claim actually asks

Andromeda and its small companion M32 both carry iron's own forest of absorption lines, Fe I —
matching that one line between two unrelated old stellar populations identifies the metal in both,
non-destructively, the same way a heated lab sample and a catalogue match would. M32's own missing
H-alpha (see [[sky/object_m32]]) is the honest contrast that makes the pairing more than a
coincidence: a spiral's disc still forms stars; a compact elliptical does not.

## The stages

Both objects are already within reach; decode each in turn. The second stage asks you to own a real
ground iron filter, so the line is yours to find again on any future target. The third spends two
exposed plates to close the case.
