NGC 7293 is what a star like the Sun eventually becomes: not an explosion, but a slow exhale. Its
outer layers have already drifted outward as an expanding shell, glowing where they are, and what is
left of the star itself sits at the centre as an exposed, superheated core — a white dwarf, visible
as a single pinpoint straight through the ring's own hole.

## A planetary nebula, not a nursery

Despite the name (a historical accident — early telescopes made these look vaguely like planets),
this has nothing to do with planet formation. It is the opposite end of a star's life from Orion's
own nursery, and yet the shell shows the identical two real lines Orion does — H-alpha and the
forbidden [O III] — because both a young ionising star and an old white dwarf can strip electrons
off the same hydrogen and oxygen the same way.

## The claim this makes possible

Reading H-alpha here and in Orion, from two objects that could not be more different in origin, is
the actual evidence behind [[research/claim_same_elements]] — the same line, from opposite ends of a
star's life, still means the same element.
