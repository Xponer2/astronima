M31 is not a cloud of gas around one star system — it is an entire second galaxy, the nearest large
one to our own, and what reaches a telescope is the combined light of hundreds of billions of stars
at once.

## An old population, mostly

The light is dominated by the galaxy's bulge: a great many cool, ageing stars whose combined
spectrum looks a great deal like the Sun's own — the same iron, sodium and calcium absorption lines
a heated lab sample would show. Its disc, unlike its bulge, still forms stars, which is why Andromeda
alone — unlike its own companion M32 — shows a trace of H-alpha too.

## Approaching, not receding

Almost every distant galaxy is redshifted, moving away as the universe expands. Andromeda is one of
the rare exceptions: it is genuinely blueshifted, falling toward us. Its own spectral lines sit
shifted the opposite way from what you would expect of a typical galaxy — a real fact, reflected in
how far its lines sit from their catalogue position, not an invented one.

## The claim this makes possible

Andromeda and its small companion M32 carry an almost identical old-star spectrum — see
[[research/claim_metal_assay]] for what matching a metal's line between the two actually proves.
