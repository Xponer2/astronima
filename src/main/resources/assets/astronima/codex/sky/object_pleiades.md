M45, the Pleiades, looks like a nebula wrapped around a handful of blue-white stars — and unlike
Orion, that is almost exactly what it is doing, not producing light of its own.

## A reflection nebula, not an emission one

There is no ionised gas here to glow on its own. The haze is ordinary interstellar dust, and what
you are seeing is starlight from the cluster's own hot young stars — Alcyone and its neighbours —
scattered off that dust the same way Earth's own sky turns blue: shorter wavelengths scatter more
efficiently, so the haze reads cooler and bluer than the stars themselves. No hydrogen line shows up
here, because nothing is being ionised — only reflected.

## Why it needs a better lens

A reflection nebula's own light is scattered starlight, not a bright emission line, so it is
genuinely fainter and more diffuse than a source like Orion. That is a real optical fact, not a
gameplay gate: a small lens simply gathers too little of that scattered light to resolve it at all.
