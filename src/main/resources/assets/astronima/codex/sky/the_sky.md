Step outside at noon and look up. The Sun is there — small, fierce, unbearable to look at — and
around it, **the stars are still out**. Not faint. Not washed away. There, at midday, exactly as
bright as they are at midnight.

That is not a trick of this place. That is what every sky looks like without air.

## Why your old sky was blue

Earth's sky is blue at noon and orange at dusk because air scatters sunlight sideways into your
eye — short wavelengths most, which is the blue, and along a low sun's longer path through the
atmosphere the blue has scattered out before it reaches you, which is the orange. All of it is the
*air* doing the work, not the sky.

Take the air away and nothing scatters. Sunlight goes past you in a straight line and only reaches
your eye if you look directly at its source. So:

- The sky is **black at noon**, and the stars never go anywhere.
- There is **no dawn and no dusk**. No gradient, no warm light on the horizon. The terminator is a
  hard line and day cuts to night as it passes.
- **Shadows are absolute.** No sky glow fills them in. What the Sun does not directly light is
  simply dark.

## The rock turns, and not conveniently

Watch long enough and the whole star field wheels — but not around a pole overhead the way Earth's
does. This body tumbles about a tilted axis, and the Sun crosses on its own separate track. Two
motions, not one.

That is worth knowing before you build anything that cares where the Sun is. A solar array sited by
one afternoon's shadows will not stay sited.

## What is up there, and worth pointing something at

Along one band of the sky the stars crowd together into a broad, structured haze — that is the
galaxy seen edge-on from inside it, the same view you always had, only now nothing is in the way of
it. Scattered against it are a handful of objects that are not stars: patches with real shape and
real colour.

Those are targets. {item:astronima:spectrograph} reads them the same way it reads the Sun, and each
one is made of something different — see [[spectroscopy/lines|Every light has a fingerprint]].
