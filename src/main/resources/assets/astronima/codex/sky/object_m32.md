M32 sits close enough beside Andromeda to share a telescope field, and through a plain eyepiece the
two are easy to mistake for one object. They are not: M32 is Andromeda's own compact elliptical
companion, a wholly separate galaxy, gravitationally bound to it.

## The same old stars, none of the young ones

Like Andromeda's bulge, M32 is dominated by old, cool stars — the identical iron, sodium and calcium
lines. What it does **not** show is the one line Andromeda's own disc does: H-alpha. A compact
elliptical like this has essentially no cold gas left to form new stars from, so there is nothing
here for an ionising young star to light up. That absence is not a gap in the data — it is the
actual, honest scientific difference between the two objects.

## Why the pair matters

Matching iron's own line, Fe I, between Andromeda's bulge and M32's own light — two unrelated old
stellar populations — identifies the element in both without ever touching either one. That is the
whole case behind [[research/claim_metal_assay]], and the missing H-alpha here is exactly what makes
the pairing more than a coincidence: it is a real, contrasting fact about two real galaxies.
