This is not an infinite plane with a bit of rock on top of it. Walk far enough in any direction
and the ground runs out — the asteroid is a real, bounded body, and you are standing inside it,
not on top of an endless one.

## It has a centre, and you are not near it

The body is **oblate** — flattened, like a spinning top rather than a perfect ball. That shape is
not decoration: a rubble pile spinning fast enough genuinely flattens itself this way, the same
reason real near-Earth asteroids like Bennu and Ryugu are shaped the way they are. Loose material
migrates toward the equator as the body spins, and over enough time that migration *is* the
flattening.

> Walk outward and the ground keeps falling away beneath you, gently at first, then more sharply
> near the edge. That fall is the body's own curve, not a hill.

## Gravity thins as you go out

You will feel this before you can measure it. Near the middle of the rock, weight is what you are
used to. Walk toward the rim and jumps carry further, a stumble goes further, and eventually
standing still at all takes real effort.

That is not a bug in how far you have walked. A spinning body's *effective* gravity is real
surface gravity minus the centrifugal pull of its own spin — full strength near the poles,
falling away toward the equator, the same physics that makes the body oblate in the first place.
Magnetic boots exist for exactly this reason: near the rim, gripping the deck stops being a
convenience and starts being the only way to stand there at all.

## The ground gets richer as you go in

Ore is not spread evenly through the rock. A differentiated body concentrates metal toward its
own centre — that is what differentiation *is* — so the deeper and more central you dig, the
better the ground gets. A vein you stripped near the surface is not the same rock you would find
by going in further.

:::locked studied:reached-the-rim
## How far it actually goes

Measured, not guessed: the body runs about **240 blocks** out from its centre at the equator and
about **80 blocks** from its centre at the poles — a flattening of roughly **3 to 1**. Its centre
sits well below where you woke up, and well below anything you have dug to yet.

There is nothing built at the centre that anyone has found. Whatever put this body together in
the first place, it did that a very long time ago.
:::

:::locked crafted:astronima:hematite_ore
## Where hematite actually sits

Hematite bands roughly the outer three-fifths of the body, measured from the centre out — well
short of the surface skin, but nowhere near the deep interior either. That is why it is the ore
you find first: common, and not particularly hard to reach.
:::

:::locked crafted:astronima:ilmenite_ore
## Where ilmenite actually sits

Ilmenite sits deeper than hematite — inside roughly the inner half of the body's own radius,
measured from the centre. Finding it always means having already dug past the ground hematite
occupies; there is no shortcut around that order.
:::

:::locked crafted:astronima:metal_rich_ore
## Why a seam is never a fluke

A metal-rich seam is real, not a lucky roll on an empty rock: differentiation genuinely
concentrates native metal this way, and every seam you find is drawn from the same real geometry
as every ore band above.

The game also guarantees a floor under how much of each ore actually exists across the whole
body — a real check run once, against the body's own measured volume, that raises how often an
ore appears only if the body's own shape would otherwise make it too scarce to find at all. At
this body's own size, that check has never actually had to raise anything: there was already
enough of everything, honestly, without it. It exists for the day the body's shape changes and
nobody remembers to re-check the arithmetic by hand.
:::

Everything above the rock's own surface, and how the sky above it behaves, is
[[sky/the_sky|a different page]] — this one is about what is under your feet.
