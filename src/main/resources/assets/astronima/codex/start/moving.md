The whole content of low gravity is not that you can jump higher. It is that **you cannot stop**.

A jump is a commitment the moment you make it. There is no air to slow you, nothing to push
against, and no second chance to change your mind halfway. The hazard of this phase is your own
momentum, and it is the only thing out here that will kill you faster than the suit runs out.

## Falls are safe, until they are very much not

![Fall distance along the bottom, damage up the side. Flat and green until about fourteen blocks, then a red quadratic climb to a full bar at around sixty-five. The dashed line is where it first costs anything; the mark near the left is where your own jump lands.](astronima:textures/codex/fall.png 160x104)

Two things shape that curve, and both are physics rather than tuning.

**Nothing is charged until the speed Minecraft already forgives.** On Earth the game absolves a
three-block fall. That is a *speed*, not a distance — and expressed as a speed it is
gravity-independent, so the same impact energy is forgiven here. Because gravity is weaker, that
same speed now takes about **fourteen blocks** to reach. The extra distance is a consequence, not a
gift.

**Past it, damage goes as the square of speed,** because kinetic energy does. The deep shaft is not
twice as bad as the shallow one; it is four times. A full bar arrives at around sixty-five blocks.

> **There is no terminal velocity in vacuum.** Nothing slows a falling body here, so a shaft goes
> on accelerating you for as long as it is deep. This is exactly backwards from the intuition low
> gravity gives you: people expect falls to be safe. Short ones are. The mineshaft is not.

The marked point is where your own jump lands — comfortably inside the free band, and deliberately
so. A phase whose entire reward is movement must not punish the movement it just handed out.

## Two problems, two answers

Low gravity has two separate problems, and an aid for one does nothing for the other.

**You cannot stay on the deck.** That is a *standing* problem, and the answer is
{item:astronima:magnetic_boots} **magnetic boots**. They grip worked iron-nickel and nothing else —
which is why they work here and would not on the ISS, where the hull is aluminium. Yours works
because this mod's entire metallurgy is iron-nickel: the plate you walked on came out of the ore
chain three tiers ago.

> Boots cost you speed while they are gripping — peeling a magnet off steel with every step is
> laborious, and an aid that were strictly better than not wearing it would not be a decision.
> Standing on bare rock they cost nothing, because they are not helping.

**You pushed off and cannot stop.** That is a *travelling* problem, and the answer is something to
**catch hold of**. A caught arrival costs nothing at all — not "reduced": an astronaut who catches
a handrail does not take a fractional injury, they have hold of it.

The price of rails is paid in **foresight and iron**. They only help on a route you prepared, so
the shaft you did not line is a planning failure and reads as one.

## What to do about it, in order

1. **Line the shaft before you use it.** Every time. The one you were only going down once is the
   one that kills you.
2. **Look before you push off.** You are choosing where you stop, not where you start.
3. **Wear the boots for work, not for travel.** Gripping is for standing still and using your
   hands; it is a bad way to cross a room.

Air is the other clock running the whole time — see [[start/breathing]]. What the suit does and
does not protect is [[start/suit]].
